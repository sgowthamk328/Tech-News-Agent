## The biggest modding site in PC gaming is taking a stance on AI

**Category:** Policy / Security  |  **Found via:** The Verge

Nexus Mods, the largest repository for PC video game modifications, has instituted a new platform policy that makes AI disclosure mandatory for all newly uploaded mods. Under these rules, creators must explicitly state whether artificial intelligence tools were used during the creation process. For content categorized under the 'AI-assisted' tag, uploaders are required to provide a clear public explanation detailing exactly how and where generative AI was utilized, such as for script writing, asset creation, texture generation, or voice synthesis. Additionally, the platform is updated to make these AI disclosures far more prominent on mod store pages, ensuring users can clearly see the origin of content before downloading.

This policy shift directly impacts two major groups: community content creators and the millions of gamers who download modifications. Mod authors must now integrate formal disclosures into their publication workflow and accommodate greater transparency around their design tools. For gamers, the change addresses ongoing community concerns regarding undisclosed synthetic content, allowing users to make informed choices about whether they want to install AI-generated materials into their games.

Moving forward, Nexus Mods plans to enforce these mandatory tags across all incoming submissions without imposing a blanket ban on AI tools. Platform leadership emphasized that banning AI outright would simply drive disclosures underground rather than stopping usage, as machine learning tools are increasingly embedded across modern software development pipelines. By maintaining a open, regulated tagging system, Nexus Mods aims to foster transparency while upholding its pro-human creative values. The platform has also reaffirmed its commitment not to sell, license, or allow any uploaded user mods to be used for training third-party machine learning models.

Who's involved: Nexus Mods, Victor "Foledinho" Folmann (Nexus Mods Owner), and PC gaming mod creators.

## Aussie Adobe assailant Canva announces layoffs in re-org affecting 22% of staff

**Category:** Industry News  |  **Found via:** The Register

Australian graphic design platform Canva has announced a major organizational restructuring affecting approximately 1,271 employees, representing 22 percent of its global workforce. The restructuring includes job cuts, role transitions, and relocation requests aimed at streamlining company operations. CEO Melanie Perkins informed staff that while Canva previously expanded rapidly to capture market share against established software giants like Adobe, its growth rate recently slowed for the first time in a decade. In response, Canva is consolidating its product, growth, and platform divisions into a unified 'Product Org' designed to reduce organizational layers, eliminate overlapping responsibilities, and restore smaller, agile decision-making teams.

This decision carries significant implications for the broader software-as-a-service (SaaS) industry and tech talent markets. Canva has long been considered one of the tech sector's brightest success stories, reporting $3.5 billion in revenue and 260 million active monthly users while remaining profitable on a free-cash-flow basis. A substantial retrenchment at such a mature, highly capitalized market leader signals that software companies are facing macro headwinds and shifting customer demands. As generative AI and new creation tools alter user expectations, SaaS providers are finding that organizational bloat accumulated during high-growth years must be trimmed to maintain operational speed.

As part of the reorganization, Canva is encouraging stronger central collaboration by relocating a larger portion of its core product development teams back to its global headquarters in Sydney, Australia. The company is offering affected workers severance packages including at least six months of pay, extended health benefits, and career transition assistance. The move mirrors broader trends across the software landscape, where prominent enterprise and consumer tech providers are realigning personnel away from legacy growth units to focus resources on leaner, next-generation product platforms.

Who's involved: Canva, Melanie Perkins (CEO and Co-founder), and Adobe.

## Anthropic is working on a system to prove AI models’ identity

**Category:** Technical Release  |  **Found via:** TechCrunch

In the artificial intelligence ecosystem, users relying on cloud-hosted models currently have no cryptographic guarantee that a prompt was answered by the specific model version they paid for or requested. An AI provider could theoretically substitute a cheaper, lower-performing model to save on compute costs or use an unverified model variant without the user's knowledge. To address this lack of transparency, Anthropic has introduced an open-source system named FlashProve designed to achieve provable inference.

At its core, FlashProve acts like a cryptographic signature system tailored specifically for deep learning outputs. When an AI company releases a model, it generates and publishes a unique mathematical fingerprint derived from the model. When a user sends a prompt and receives a response, FlashProve generates a lightweight cryptographic proof alongside the output. A user can then locally run a verification algorithm to check that the proof matches the published model fingerprint. Crucially, this mechanism operates without revealing the underlying model weights—the proprietary numerical parameters that define how a neural network functions. This zero-knowledge design allows commercial AI developers to offer verifiable proof of model identity while keeping their valuable proprietary intellectual property fully protected.

This framework solves several pressing problems in digital trust and compliance. First, it prevents unauthorized model swapping by cloud hosting services or AI vendors, ensuring consumers and enterprise clients receive answers from the exact model specified in their service-level agreements. Second, it enables third parties to audit automated claims—such as a company proving that a software codebase was audited by a specific, highly capable cybersecurity model before release. Finally, the verification computation is intentionally lightweight, allowing clients to confirm output authenticity on local devices without relying on additional centralized trust authorities.

While Anthropic has released the technical specification and code on GitHub under an open-source license, FlashProve remains an ongoing research initiative rather than a live feature deployed across active consumer products.

Who's involved: Anthropic, FlashProve, and open-source AI developers.

## California fights "preposterous" Trump admin demand to scrap state broadband laws

**Category:** Policy / Security  |  **Found via:** Ars Technica

A major policy conflict has erupted between federal broadband administrators and state regulators over consumer protection laws. The federal National Telecommunications and Information Administration (NTIA), operating under the Trump administration, introduced a rule known as Section 50 within its $42 billion Broadband Equity, Access, and Deployment (BEAD) grant program. This rule conditions federal infrastructure funding on a state's agreement to exempt participating internet service providers (ISPs) from local rate regulations, net neutrality rules, data throttling bans, and utility-style oversight for up to 14 years. California, slated to receive $1.42 billion to expand high-speed internet to over 270,000 unserved locations, formally accepted the award but submitted an explicit legal reservation rejecting the federal mandate to strip away its state regulations.

This regulatory clash directly affects internet service providers, California consumers, and emergency responders. California enforces strict state-level net neutrality laws that prohibit ISPs from blocking content, throttling internet speeds, or charging websites extra fees for fast-lane delivery. The state also maintains explicit prohibitions against throttling data for first responders during natural disasters—a policy enacted after emergency services faced data slowdowns during major wildfires. Under the NTIA's conditions, ISPs receiving federal BEAD subgrants would be legally immune from state enforcement of these protections across their entire operational network in the state, not just in funded expansion zones.

What happens next is a potential freeze in infrastructure distribution followed by high-stakes litigation. The California Public Utilities Commission (CPUC) announced that while it has signed the overall federal agreement, it will not distribute any subgrant funds to internet service providers until it resolves the constitutional disagreement with federal officials. The NTIA has maintained that its award conditions are non-negotiable and that state reservation letters hold no legal weight. If the NTIA attempts to withhold funds or claw back grants, California is positioned to file a federal lawsuit challenging Section 50 as an unconstitutional infringement on state sovereignty and an overreach beyond the Congressional statute that established the BEAD program.

Who's involved: California Public Utilities Commission (CPUC), National Telecommunications and Information Administration (NTIA), CPUC President John Reynolds, California Governor Gavin Newsom, and broadband Internet Service Providers (ISPs).
