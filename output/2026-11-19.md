## Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella explains why he asked 60K employees to quit

**Category:** Industry News  |  **Found via:** TechCrunch

Microsoft chief executive Satya Nadella publicly addressed his decision to offer voluntary buyout packages to roughly 60,000 employees. The sweeping move represents a fundamental operational pivot driven by the rise of artificial intelligence, but Nadella stressed that the underlying goal is not straightforward workforce replacement. Instead, Microsoft is aggressively restructuring its internal workflows so that human workers transition from directly executing specialized tasks to managing autonomous AI systems. 

Nadella illustrated this shift using software engineering as an example. Rather than eliminating human coders entirely, Microsoft is moving toward an operating model where engineers direct and orchestrate AI tools rather than manually writing code line by line. To demonstrate, he cited the 11-person developer group behind Cowork—an AI assistant within Microsoft 365—describing them as builders of a 'software factory' that produces software, rather than builders of the software product itself. In Nadella's view, this transition toward supervising agentic AI will eventually define virtually every corporate role.

Because adapting to this new paradigm requires significant operational upheaval, Microsoft chose voluntary buyouts over involuntary layoffs to give staff members agency. Longstanding legacy enterprises face structural inertia when transforming workflows, culture, and compensation models to become AI-first. Acknowledging widespread worker anxiety sparked by hyperbolic warnings regarding artificial general intelligence and job loss, Nadella framed the buyout program as a transparent off-ramp. Employees unwilling or unable to transition into managing autonomous software agents are being given an incentive to depart voluntarily, setting a notable precedent for how major technology incumbents manage the human cost of AI adoption.

Who's involved: Satya Nadella (CEO of Microsoft), Microsoft, Andrew Ross Sorkin (DealBook Summit interviewer), Gergely Orosz (The Pragmatic Engineer podcast host).

## Chinese web giant Tencent says new US GPU ban ‘sets everyone back in China’

**Category:** Policy / Security  |  **Found via:** The Register

United States export regulators have expanded restrictions by barring Chinese companies from remotely accessing advanced graphics processing units (GPUs) housed in overseas data centers, closing an off-premise loophole previously used to bypass physical chip export embargoes. This policy change directly affects Chinese technology enterprises, cloud providers, and artificial intelligence developers who relied on remote leasing arrangements to train cutting-edge foundation models. Under the new rules, access to top-tier international computing power is cut off, forcing firms to halt external high-end GPU leasing and rethink their multi-region infrastructure architectures. Martin Lau, president of Chinese tech giant Tencent, noted during the company's Q3 earnings call that while current model inference workloads and everyday applications can largely be sustained using domestically produced semiconductors, the training of next-generation frontier models will face immediate delays due to the scarcity of high-throughput hardware. The restriction also curtails Tencent's ability to offer high-end GPU rental services to external cloud customers. Looking ahead, the policy will catalyze two parallel developments. In the near term, Chinese firms will likely experience slower research cadences for complex artificial intelligence architectures like large-scale foundation models. Over the long term, however, the restriction is expected to aggressively drive domestic capital, engineering resources, and software optimization toward homegrown semiconductor fabricators and domestic supply chains to achieve self-sufficiency in high-performance computing hardware.

Who's involved: Tencent, Martin Lau (President of Tencent), James Mitchell (Chief Strategy Officer of Tencent), and the United States Government.

## Amidst fears of AI cyberattacks, Satya Nadella says lots of critical infrastructure is ‘unpatchable’

**Category:** Industry News  |  **Found via:** TechCrunch

Speaking at The New York Times' DealBook Summit, Microsoft Chief Executive Officer Satya Nadella highlighted an escalating systemic vulnerability across global networks: vast segments of modern critical infrastructure rely on legacy hardware, cooling systems, and transportation firmware that cannot be patched remotely despite being connected directly to the public internet. This warning arrives amid growing friction between offense and defense in cybersecurity. Machine learning algorithms and automated vulnerability tools now discover zero-day exploits at speeds that far outpace traditional human testing, validation, and staging workflows. When modern security teams are forced to push remediation code directly to live production environments to counter active AI-assisted exploits, the compressed testing window frequently causes collateral instability, triggering unintended outages in interconnected software supply chains. Nadella’s remarks place recent high-profile system disruptions, including Microsoft Outlook and aviation IT failures, into a wider industry context where the root challenge is not merely malicious intrusion, but the fragility created when hyperscale patching collides with brittle legacy operational technology. For the broader technology and enterprise landscape, this dynamic implies that cybersecurity strategies can no longer focus solely on perimeter defense or reactive hotfixes. Instead, organizations and infrastructure operators face an urgent imperative to redesign basic system architectures, build upstream operational resilience, and create runtime environments capable of isolating unpatchable embedded devices before automated offensive tools can systematically exploit them across public networks.

Who's involved: Microsoft, Satya Nadella (CEO of Microsoft), and The New York Times DealBook Summit.

## Ted Cruz-led committee approves Trump FCC nominee 15-0 as Democrats boycott vote

**Category:** Policy / Security  |  **Found via:** Ars Technica

The Senate Commerce Committee, chaired by Senator Ted Cruz, advanced the nomination of Danielle Thumann to the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) with a 15-0 party-line vote. The vote proceeded despite a complete boycott by all 13 committee Democrats, who staged the walkout to deny a quorum and protest both the nominee's candor and the unilateral partisan process.

The underlying dispute centers on procedural traditions and allegations of misleading testimony. Democrats contended that under committee norms dating back to 2005, official votes require the physical presence of at least one minority member. Cruz overrode this objection by citing broader Standing Rules of the Senate, arguing they contain provisions allowing a majority vote to report nominations to the full floor regardless of internal committee rules. Democrats also raised ethical flags, claiming Thumann gave false testimony when denying involvement in media-related files during her tenure advising current FCC Chairman Brendan Carr—specifically citing FOIA records showing her processing correspondence on a CBS 60 Minutes controversy and merger commitments involving Paramount and Skydance. 

If confirmed by the full Senate, Thumann's appointment will grant Republicans a 3-1 working majority on the five-seat commission, with sole Democratic commissioner Anna Gomez facing an expiring term in late 2027. This solidifies conservative control over major federal communications policy, including potential rollbacks of net neutrality regulations, media ownership restrictions, and broadcast licensing oversight. Next, the full Senate will schedule a confirmation vote, where Republicans aim to secure Thumann's seat before an upcoming congressional transition, even as Democrats threaten to contest the procedural validity of the committee's reporting.

Who's involved: Senator Ted Cruz, FCC nominee Danielle Thumann, FCC Chairman Brendan Carr, Senator Maria Cantwell, and Senator Ben Ray Luján.

## The nation’s largest school districts consider ditching Google Search

**Category:** Policy / Security  |  **Found via:** The Verge

Major United States public school districts—including New York City, Los Angeles, and Chicago—are actively considering dropping Google Search after the tech giant revealed that schools opting out of its artificial intelligence tools will lose access to its web search engine entirely. Google notified administrators that starting next year, Gemini-driven generative features and classic search functionality will be inextricably bundled, eliminating the ability to turn off AI overviews while keeping basic indexing active.

This policy shift directly impacts millions of primary and secondary school students and educators who depend on Google's institutional software ecosystem. District leaders and local policymakers view generative AI as a profound disruption to student learning, citing risks that synthetic answer engines undermine foundational critical thinking, encourage automated plagiarism, and present untested mental health consequences for minors. In response to Google's all-or-nothing approach, public officials, including New York City schools chancellor Kamar Samuels, stated their intention to pilot competing search engines that permit strict parental and administrative controls without AI overlays.

Google has attempted to calm the backlash by announcing "Guided Learning" modules designed to coach students through homework step-by-step rather than outputting immediate answers, alongside promises that certain Gemini apps will remain off by default until administrators approve them. However, school districts are currently auditing their operational infrastructure. What happens next depends on district procurement negotiations: if Google does not grant schools a clean, AI-free version of standard Search, several of the nation's largest municipal districts are prepared to begin transitioning off Google's institutional products, testing whether public school systems can realistically decouple from ubiquitous commercial platforms like Chromebooks and Google Classroom.

Who's involved: Google, Google VP of Education Chris Phillips, New York City Department of Education, and public school systems in New York City, Los Angeles, and Chicago.

## Ukraine blew up Putin’s bridge using relay drones to bypass Starlink limits

**Category:** Technical Release  |  **Found via:** Ars Technica

Modern precision warfare heavily relies on continuous, low-latency data links between remote operators and strike systems. When guided missiles or drones fly long distances into enemy territory, operators require real-time telemetry and video feeds to bypass electronic countermeasures and pinpoint high-value structural targets. While satellite links like SpaceX's Starlink or military-grade Starshield traditionally supply this connectivity, their operational constraints—such as geopolitical restrictions or built-in limits on fast-moving craft—create severe operational blind spots.

To overcome these constraints during deep strikes against Russia's Kerch Strait Bridge, Ukrainian forces deployed an airborne radio mesh network using relay drones. The architecture works by launching a distributed series of aerial drones equipped with specialized mesh radio transceivers along a designated corridor. In a traditional point-to-point wireless setup, range is bound by line-of-sight and ground obstacles, and signal strength degrades rapidly over distance. In a mesh configuration, each drone acts as an autonomous relay node that receives, amplifies, and forwards data packets to the next node in the chain.

By chaining these airborne relays together, the military established an ad-hoc high-bandwidth communication pipeline stretching more than 250 kilometers (155 miles). This flying pipeline carried bidirectional command-and-control signals directly from human operators in safe territory to remote-controlled weapons, including Ukraine's domestically produced Flamingo cruise missiles and strike drones. The dynamic aerial relay provided enough reliable bandwidth for operators to actively evade local Russian air defenses and guide the missiles into the bridge's most vulnerable structural supports, successfully collapsing a key roadway arch.

This implementation solves the critical bottleneck of external satellite dependence by using decentralized, locally controlled field hardware. It decouples long-range precision strikes from third-party satellite networks, demonstrating how cheap off-the-shelf and military mesh networking can restore operational autonomy in contested environments where satellite coverage is throttled, spoofed, or politically restricted.

Who's involved: Ukrainian Armed Forces, SpaceX (Starshield), Institute for the Study of War, and Vladimir Putin.

## Get ready for another critical PHP security release to drop

**Category:** Policy / Security  |  **Found via:** The Register

The PHP Foundation announced an impending out-of-band security update scheduled to address multiple high-severity vulnerabilities in the PHP runtime, including at least one flaw that permits remote code execution (RCE). Tracked partly under CVE-2026-115560, the issues require targeted configuration states to exploit, which led developers to categorize them as critical while planning an orderly patch rather than an emergency zero-day stopgap.

This advisory directly affects system administrators, DevOps engineers, hosting providers, and patch management teams responsible for the vast footprint of the web—approximately 70 percent of modern websites utilize PHP in their server-side software stack. In CVE-2026-115560, the vulnerability stems from handling session state deserialization. If an application alters its serialization handler to an insecure format at runtime before starting a session, and simultaneously enables session upload progress monitoring, external attackers could manipulate incoming data payloads to execute unauthorized code directly on the host server. The release also addresses wider vulnerabilities surfaced following recent high-profile flaws dubbed 'PHP2Shell.'

Organizations must prepare for immediate deployment once the official binaries and package updates are published by their respective Linux distributions and PHP maintainers. Prior to installing the patches, administrators who cannot upgrade instantly have been advised to apply a mitigation policy: explicitly disabling session upload progress by setting 'session.upload_progress.enabled = 0' within their php.ini configuration files. Following release, enterprise security teams must audit their environments to ensure configuration defaults have not been tampered with and that runtime overrides cannot be manipulated by untrusted requests. Because previous PHP flaws were rapidly added to CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog after being reverse-engineered and exploited by threat groups, failure to apply these updates leaves servers vulnerable to automated network compromise.

Who's involved: The PHP Foundation, Elizabeth Barron (Executive Director), CISA, and enterprise web administrators.

**Source:** https://www.theregister.com/patches/2026/11/18/get-ready-for-another-critical-php-security-release-to-drop/5310911
