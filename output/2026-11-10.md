## Otter AI can now follow conversations across 30+ languages

**Category:** Technical Release  |  **Found via:** TechCrunch

Otter.ai has expanded its automated transcription platform by introducing multilingual support for 31 languages, along with a feature designed to handle mid-conversation language switching. Automatically capturing meeting notes when participants transition fluidly between different languages—such as moving from English to Korean within a single sentence or agenda item—presents a complex technical challenge. Traditional speech-to-text systems typically rely on pre-selected language settings or fixed single-language acoustic models, which fail or degrade significantly when faced with multilingual audio streams.

To solve this, Otter built proprietary speech recognition models trained on publicly available datasets and refined through evaluations with native-speaking volunteers. These models continuously analyze incoming audio signals to perform dynamic language detection and automatic speech recognition (ASR) without manual intervention. By automatically identifying language transitions in real time, the system maintains accurate transcription and speaker attribution across international team discussions. This release directly addresses friction in global enterprise communication, ensuring that organizations can maintain comprehensive, automated meeting records without forcing participants to restrict themselves to a single primary language.

This update is part of Otter's broader strategy to expand its product footprint beyond basic transcription into real-time meeting assistance. By pairing dynamic multilingual processing with workflow integration features like Work Mode and Live Assist, the company aims to embed its AI tools deeper into corporate operations. The initial rollout includes 13 languages, with an additional 14 scheduled for release, reinforcing the infrastructure required for enterprise-grade voice AI.

Who's involved: Otter.ai, General Catalyst.

## California’s proposed ‘billionaire tax’ fails to pass

**Category:** Policy / Security  |  **Found via:** TechCrunch

California voters rejected Proposition 40, a proposed state ballot initiative that would have established a one-time 5% wealth tax on California residents with a net worth exceeding $1 billion. Designed as a retroactive levy based on residency as of January 1, 2026, the tax aimed to generate an estimated $100 billion over five years to fund public healthcare programs like Medi-Cal and educational initiatives.

With the rejection of Proposition 40, California's tax architecture remains unchanged for its wealthiest citizens. Ultra-high-net-worth individuals, tech founders, and venture capitalists will not face immediate asset dilution or mandated liquidations of illiquid corporate stock to satisfy a state wealth tax. Silicon Valley business leaders and investors strongly opposed the measure, arguing it would have triggered severe capital flight, disincentivized high-growth entrepreneurship, and forced founders to relinquish voting control in their companies to pay tax obligations. Opponents, backed by high-profile tech figures like Google co-founder Sergey Brin, spent roughly $379 million to defeat the measure—outspending labor union backers by a 10-to-1 ratio.

Following the vote, tech hub states like California retain their current tax revenues derived from capital gains and top-bracket income taxes, avoiding the immediate risk of billionaire relocations to lower-tax states like Florida or Texas. However, public policy advocates and labor unions behind Proposition 40 have indicated that the economic debate over taxing unrealized tech wealth is not over, signaling potential plans to introduce refined wealth tax measures on future state ballots in 2028.

Who's involved: State of California voters, California Labor Unions, Sergey Brin, Emmanuel Saez (UC Berkeley), Gabriel Zucman (UC Berkeley), Nic Carter (Castle Island Ventures), Jesse Tinsley (Mainstreet).

## Anthropic's super bug-finding Mythos model is going into Cisco and IBM products

**Category:** Industry News  |  **Found via:** The Register

Anthropic has entered commercial partnerships with Cisco and IBM to embed its highly specialized security model, Mythos, directly into mainstream enterprise software tools. Mythos is an advanced artificial intelligence model specifically designed for cyber-reasoning and automated software vulnerability discovery. Because autonomous vulnerability-detection tools pose significant dual-use security risks—meaning malicious actors could use them to discover zero-day exploits before software vendors can patch them—Anthropic initially limited access to vetted institutions under controlled initiatives like "Project Glasswing."

Under the new commercial framework, Cisco is incorporating Mythos capabilities into its AI Defense platform to perform automated red-teaming and stress-testing on customer AI models and applications. Similarly, IBM is integrating Mythos into its Concert security suite to uncover code vulnerabilities that conventional static scanners miss, assess their real-world risk, and draft automated fixes for developer review. Crucially, these integrations allow enterprise customers to benefit from Mythos's underlying bug-hunting capabilities via API calls without giving end users direct access to raw model weights or freeform prompts. This proxy deployment model enables Anthropic to meet widespread enterprise demand for advanced AI auditing while maintaining strict guardrails against safety threats and intellectual property theft.

Alongside these enterprise platform deals, Anthropic announced updated 5.2 versions of its Mythos and Fable models, claiming up to 30 percent faster output generation, lower token pricing, and improved defense against model distillation attacks (a practice where rival developers use one model's outputs to train competing systems).

Who's involved: Anthropic, Cisco (specifically its AI Defense product), and IBM (specifically its Concert security platform).

## France blames Iran for supermarket hack meltdowns

**Category:** Policy / Security  |  **Found via:** The Register

France has formally attributed a widespread series of destructive cyberattacks targeting national grocery chain infrastructure to Iran's state-sponsored hackers. In October, cyber intruders breached industrial control systems managing commercial refrigeration units across more than 700 supermarkets. By remotely altering temperature settings—in some cases pushing temperatures to 122°F (50°C)—the attackers caused massive food spoilage, triggered at least 15 structural fires, and generated tens of millions of euros in financial losses. French authorities characterized the campaign as the country's first cyber offensive on domestic soil carried out exclusively for physical sabotage.

Investigations led by France’s Cyber Crisis Coordination Centre (C4) identified the perpetrator as the Shahid Kaveh cyber unit, an operational branch of Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) frequently operating under the public handle CyberAv3ngers. Technical evidence connecting the group includes shared attack infrastructure previously used to probe industrial control systems, lingering digital traces of Farsi language in command systems, and rapid publicity of the breaches by Iranian state-linked media channels. Similar temperature-manipulation attacks subsequently impacted supermarket chains in Belgium, Ireland, Luxembourg, and Sweden, prompting international cybersecurity agencies to warn critical infrastructure operators to isolate operational technology from public internet exposure.

In response to these malicious intrusions, France is escalating diplomatic and security countermeasures. The French government announced it will push for expanded European Union sanctions against the Iranian individuals and organizations involved in the operation. Furthermore, French authorities summoned Iran’s ambassador to deliver a formal diplomatic condemnation, while explicitly asserting the right to take additional public and undisclosed non-public measures to deter future state-sponsored aggression.

Who's involved: France's Ministry for Europe and Foreign Affairs, France's Cyber Crisis Coordination Centre (C4), Iranian Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC / Shahid Kaveh / CyberAv3ngers), Schneider Electric, Siemens, Rockwell, and French supermarket chains (Casino, Leclerc, Coopérative U, Metro, Carrefour, Intermarché).

## DCU shake-up: James Gunn and Peter Safran out post-merger

**Category:** Industry News  |  **Found via:** Ars Technica

In a major executive reshuffle following the mega-merger between Paramount and Warner Bros., DC Studios co-CEOs James Gunn and Peter Safran are stepping down from their leadership roles. The duo was originally appointed four years ago to construct a unified, interconnected creative vision across film, television, and animation for DC Comics properties, mirroring the centralized universe model popularized by Marvel Studios. Under their tenure, DC Studios launched high-profile projects such as the flagship movie reboot *Superman*, television spin-offs like *Peacemaker* and *Lanterns*, and upcoming feature releases like *Superman: Man of Tomorrow*.

Replacing Gunn and Safran is veteran producer Jeremy Latcham, a former Marvel Studios executive who worked on formative Marvel Cinematic Universe titles like *Iron Man* and *The Avengers* before producing standalone projects like *Dungeons & Dragons: Honor Among Thieves*. The administrative structure is also shifting: while Gunn and Safran previously reported directly to corporate leadership, Latcham will report to Dana Goldberg and Josh Greenstein, the co-chairs of the newly formed Skydance Motion Picture Group. Gunn plans to return exclusively to creative writing and directing, continuing his work on the *Superman* sequel while relinquishing corporate oversight.

This executive transition reflects broader post-merger adjustments within Hollywood's franchise entertainment ecosystem. Corporate consolidations often trigger leadership restructuring as new parent organizations seek to align film divisions with updated financial and strategic priorities. For DC Studios, Latcham’s appointment signals potential shifts in project greenlighting and slate planning. While Gunn favored an auteur-driven approach with offbeat humor, Latcham brings extensive experience managing large-scale studio franchises, highlighting an ongoing industry struggle between tightly controlled shared cinematic universes and standalone, creator-led entertainment.

Who's involved: DC Studios, James Gunn, Peter Safran, Jeremy Latcham, Skydance Motion Picture Group (Dana Goldberg and Josh Greenstein), and Warner Bros. Discovery / Paramount.

**Source:** https://arstechnica.com/culture/2026/11/dcu-shake-up-james-gunn-and-peter-safran-out-post-merger/

*It remains unconfirmed whether Gunn and Safran voluntarily resigned or were requested to step down by incoming corporate management.*
