## FAA lifts nationwide ground stop that blocked takeoffs for hours

**Category:** Industry News  |  **Found via:** The Verge

Imagine the United States airspace as a complex highway network where air traffic control centers use radar and radio frequencies to coordinate every airplane's precise path and altitude. On Friday afternoon, the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) issued a nationwide ground stop—only the third in U.S. history—halting all domestic departures for over four hours. Planes already airborne were permitted to land, but ground departures were completely frozen to prevent mid-air coordination failures.

The disruption stemmed from a severe telecommunications outage within the Terminal Radar Approach Control (TRACON) systems, primarily affecting centers in Chicago, Boston, Washington, New York, and Minneapolis. According to transportation officials, aging analog telecommunications equipment suffered data inconsistencies when switches and routers became overloaded. Other reports linked the incident to a private communications network update managed by defense contractor L3Harris. Officials addressed concerns regarding external threats, stating there was no indication of a cyberattack or connection to recent cloud outages, nor any proven link to the FAA's newly deployed AI airspace management systems.

While the ground stop was lifted after four hours, the ripple effects were immediate and far-reaching, resulting in over 9,000 delayed flights and nearly 6,000 cancellations nationwide. Beyond immediate travel chaos, the failure highlights critical vulnerabilities in aging civilian infrastructure. It puts pressure on regulatory agencies and governments to modernize critical hardware, upgrade private telecom routing contracts, and build better redundancy into legacy systems that manage national infrastructure.

Who's involved: The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), U.S. Department of Transportation, Air Space Intelligence (ASI), and L3Harris.

## Starship flight 15 powers to orbit—setting up a possible catch on Saturday

**Category:** Technical Release  |  **Found via:** Ars Technica

SpaceX's Starship is designed as a two-stage super-heavy launch vehicle capable of reaching low-Earth orbit with unprecedented payload capacity. Its core technological innovation lies in full, rapid reusability. Achieving this requires both the Super Heavy booster (first stage) and the Starship spacecraft (upper stage) to return safely to Earth, land precisely, and undergo minimal refurbishment between flights.

On its 15th test flight, Starship demonstrated significant progress by successfully carrying 26 next-generation Starlink V3 satellites into orbit. This verified the upper stage's mechanical deployment doors and orbital maneuvering, officially proving Starship's operational deployment capability. However, the mission also highlighted ongoing engineering challenges. The Super Heavy booster experienced an anomaly during descent, forcing automated flight systems to divert it into the Gulf of Mexico, where it exploded upon impact rather than returning to the launch tower for a mechanized catch.

The key remaining technical bottleneck for Starship's upper stage reusability is thermal protection. Re-entering Earth's atmosphere exposes the vehicle to extreme temperatures generated by atmospheric friction and plasma generation. To solve this, SpaceX upgraded the upper stage's heat shield with curved ceramic tiles and improved mechanical retention mechanisms. These updates aim to prevent tile detachment during ascent and restrict plasma flow through small gaps between tiles. Resolving these thermodynamic and mechanical stresses is essential for preserving the vehicle's structural integrity, allowing the upper stage to execute controlled atmospheric descents and tower catch maneuvers.

Who's involved: SpaceX, Starship, Super Heavy booster, and Starlink.

## Amazon’s new Kindle Colorsoft is a premium e-reader with a premium price

**Category:** Technical Release  |  **Found via:** TechCrunch

Electronic paper displays, commonly known as E-Ink, function by manipulating microscopic microcapsules suspended in liquid to reflect light just like traditional paper. Unlike standard liquid crystal displays (LCDs) or organic light-emitting diode (OLED) screens found on smartphones and tablets, E-Ink screens emit no light of their own, leading to vastly reduced eye strain and multi-week battery performance. Historically, these displays were restricted to monochromatic black-and-white output by toggling white and black pigments inside each capsule.

Amazon's hardware release, the refreshed Kindle Colorsoft, solves the fundamental visual limitation of dedicated e-readers by integrating color capabilities directly into an ultra-thin E-Ink screen stack. The device operates at a crisp 300 pixels-per-inch (ppi) resolution for standard black text, while using a color filter layer over the microcapsules to produce 150ppi color rendering. This design approach allows digital book lovers to view colored book covers, vibrant comic panels, graphic novels, and multi-colored highlighted passages while retaining the glare-free daylight readability and battery efficiency of traditional electronic paper. 

To improve ergonomics and daily usability over previous hardware revisions, the device features an edge-to-edge glass display with a thinner, lighter chassis. Engineers repositioned the physical power toggle from the bottom boundary to the top-right edge to minimize unintended power state toggles during reading sessions. Additionally, the device uses software-driven front lighting with adjustable color temperature controls, allowing readers to shift screen luminescence toward warm yellow wavelengths for nighttime reading. The premium Signature Edition variant incorporates pogo pins along the back exterior to communicate directly with active accessories like physical page-turn covers while providing double the standard internal flash storage.

Who's involved: Amazon, Kindle Colorsoft, E Ink Corporation.

Researchers analyzed data collected from over 8,000 U.S. adults to estimate overall population-level usage and compliance. The study found that nearly 10 percent of U.S. adults (roughly 27 million people) took a GLP-1 drug in 2025, but a striking 44 percent of users altered their regimen away from official medical guidance by skipping doses, reducing amounts, or delaying refills, primarily due to financial costs and supply shortages.

What makes this research novel is its systematic empirical quantification of off-label and unapproved GLP-1 usage patterns, particularly regarding compounded drugs. While medical researchers knew compounding pharmacies produced unapproved alternatives during drug shortages, the CDC data revealed that at least 23 percent of GLP-1 patients rely on compounded versions. Furthermore, patients taking compounded variants modified their dosages at far higher rates (62 percent) than those on brand-name medications (36 percent). Combined with misleading marketing on telehealth websites, where unapproved variants are frequently conflated with FDA-approved brands, the study documents a widespread gap between official clinical trial protocols and real-world execution.

These findings enable researchers and regulatory bodies to target real-world safety studies. By identifying the most common patient-driven modifications—such as micro-dosing or spacing out injections—medical agencies can design clinical studies to evaluate whether these modified schedules remain therapeutic or carry unforeseen health risks, ultimately informing future prescribing guidelines and regulatory oversight.

## Chinese AI labs are training models on Nvidia's Blackwell despite export controls, says ex-OpenAI engineer

**Category:** Research  |  **Found via:** The Register

Former OpenAI engineer Will DePue published a groundbreaking forensic investigation revealing that major Chinese artificial intelligence laboratories—including DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, MiniMax, and Ant Group—have successfully trained frontier AI models using Nvidia's restricted Blackwell-architecture graphics processing units (GPUs). Despite strict United States export controls designed to block China's access to advanced semiconductor hardware, this study provides mathematical evidence that cutting-edge Chinese models carry hardware-specific initialization traces from top-tier chips like the GB200 and GB300 liquid-cooled systems.

The novelty of this discovery lies in its clever hardware forensic method. When an AI model begins training, its neural network weights must first be initialized with pseudorandom numbers. Deep learning frameworks like PyTorch generate these starting values using an algorithm called Philox via Nvidia’s cuRAND library, which divides execution across parallel GPU threads. Because GPU architectures possess unique physical core layouts and thread distribution behaviors, initializing a model with a specific seed value on a specific chip leaves a unique mathematical "fingerprint" in the starting parameters. DePue demonstrated that subtle statistical artifacts of this initial layout persist throughout the entire training process into the final published open-weights model. By searching over six trillion combinations of seed numbers and GPU chip configurations, the researcher matched released open weights back to the exact hardware family on which they were initialized.

This forensic methodology enables an entirely new paradigm for supply chain auditing, regulatory enforcement, and AI security oversight. Historically, government agencies struggled to track whether foreign entities obtained embargoed hardware or accessed restricted compute resources through proxy cloud providers. By demonstrating that published neural network weights retain immutable physical hardware signatures, auditors and regulators now possess an objective, non-invasive mechanism to verify compliance with international export restrictions. Moving forward, this hardware fingerprinting technique will likely be adopted by cybersecurity researchers and policy enforcement bodies to monitor global AI chip proliferation, detect illicit cloud compute leasing, and verify the physical provenance of open-weights frontier models.

Who's involved: Will DePue (Unemployment Labs), DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, MiniMax, Ant Group, Nvidia.

**Source:** https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/11/13/chinese-ai-labs-are-training-models-on-nvidias-blackwell-despite-export-controls-says-ex-openai-engineer/5309891
