## FCC approves SpaceX plan to put AI data centers in space

**Category:** Policy / Security  |  **Found via:** TechCrunch

The Federal Communications Commission (FCC) has granted conditional regulatory approval to SpaceX to deploy and operate an ambitious constellation of up to 1 million satellites for orbital AI data centers, known as Starmind. This regulatory clearance represents a pivotal shift, allowing SpaceX to move forward with using radiofrequency communications to maintain control over these space-bound data centers from Earth if primary optical laser connections fail. The approval was granted despite formal petitions and objections from industry competitors like Amazon and Viasat, as well as the American Astronomical Society, who expressed serious concerns regarding space debris, orbital congestion, radio frequency interference, and potential harm to scientific astronomy. 

This decision primarily impacts satellite internet competitors, space safety researchers, and the broader commercial aerospace sector. The FCC dismissed several opposition arguments on the grounds that scientific research impacts fell outside its legal regulatory mandate. However, the authorization comes with strict timeline conditions: SpaceX is required to orbit its first operational Starmind satellite within seven years and complete the full constellation deployment within 14 years. Additionally, SpaceX must still resolve unapproved proposals regarding orbital debris management for satellites operating above 600 kilometers, where it requested a waiver from standard five-year atmospheric re-entry rules to park retired units in higher Earth or solar orbits. Before these floating data centers become operational, SpaceX must also solve formidable engineering and economic challenges, including extreme chip-cooling requirements, radiation shielding, and reducing space manufacturing costs.

Who's involved: SpaceX, Federal Communications Commission (FCC), Elon Musk, Brendan Carr, Amazon, Viasat, American Astronomical Society.

## OpenAI says heavy ChatGPT users may hit limits sooner as it merges Chat and Work

**Category:** Technical Release  |  **Found via:** TechCrunch

OpenAI has begun rolling out a major structural redesign to ChatGPT by consolidating its separate 'Chat' and 'Work' interfaces into a single, unified workspace. Under the hood, this architectural change introduces a shared usage meter across the platform rather than keeping separate caps for different functional tabs. Previously, subscribers maintained distinct allowances depending on whether they were engaged in standard conversational tasks or working within specialized workspace environments. Now, both activities draw from the exact same underlying pool of compute resources, simplifying the user interface so individuals can move seamlessly between simple queries and multi-step complex workflows without toggling contexts.

This platform consolidation solves the user-experience problem of fragmented workspaces and disparate task management, but it presents a trade-off for power users. Because high-powered reasoning models require significant computational processing, combining these activity streams into a shared allowance meter means heavy power users may reach their daily or hourly usage caps faster than before. To cushion this transition, OpenAI designed the system so that lightweight queries powered by its 'Instant' model remain completely unmetered, allowing continuous platform access even after a user's high-tier computing allocation is exhausted. This backend adjustment mirrors broader efforts across the AI industry, such as Anthropic's integration of Claude Chat and Cowork, to optimize server compute resources while streamlining complex AI agent capabilities under a single system architecture.

Who's involved: OpenAI, ChatGPT, Thibault Sottiaux, Anthropic.
