## This could mess with Thanksgiving, says VMware as it drops third critical patch bundle in a week

**Category:** Policy / Security  |  **Found via:** The Register

VMware, a business unit of Broadcom, issued a major security advisory (VMSA-2026-0011) addressing 23 vulnerabilities across ten products, including four rated as critical. This release represents VMware's third critical patch bundle within a single week, dropping right before the United States Thanksgiving holiday. The most severe flaw, designated CVE-2026-123903 with a 9.8 CVSS rating, allows unauthenticated attackers to bypass security mechanisms and gain control of vCenter, the central management console used to control virtual machines and server hosts. Other critical vulnerabilities involve severe memory-handling bugs (such as heap out-of-bounds writes and use-after-free flaws) in the ESX hypervisor that enable arbitrary remote code execution, as well as virtual machine escape flaws affecting desktop hypervisors like VMware Workstation and Fusion.

This urgent patch release affects system administrators, enterprise IT teams, and organizations reliant on VMware's virtualization infrastructure. Anyone managing vCenter, ESX, Cloud Foundation, or related networking and load-balancing tools must prioritize auditing their environments and applying software updates immediately. For certain desktop virtualization bugs involving USB controllers, administrators can apply temporary workarounds by disabling unused virtual hardware devices while preparing full software updates.

Moving forward, enterprise security teams must expedite patching cycles despite the awkward holiday timing, as public disclosure of 9.8-rated authentication bypass flaws frequently leads to rapid exploitation by malicious threat actors. The surge in reported vulnerabilities is tied in part to industry initiatives like Project Glasswing, which leverages automated AI models to identify deep software defects, signaling that enterprise software vendors will likely continue issuing larger, more frequent security update bundles in the future.

Who's involved: VMware (Broadcom), Project Glasswing, Anthropic.

## Nutanix CEO announces gradual departure, intent to complete set of storage links

**Category:** Industry News  |  **Found via:** The Register

Rajiv Ramaswami, the Chief Executive Officer of enterprise cloud software vendor Nutanix, has announced his intention to step down once the company's board selects a successor. Ramaswami joined Nutanix in 2020 after previously serving as an executive at rival VMware. During his tenure, he guided Nutanix through what he characterizes as its "second chapter"—transitioning the firm from a specialized hyperconverged infrastructure (HCI) provider focused on niche virtual desktop workloads into a profitable hybrid cloud platform capable of managing workloads across both on-premises data centers and public clouds.

This leadership transition comes at a critical juncture for the enterprise virtualization and cloud infrastructure market. Nutanix has been positioned as a primary alternative for corporate IT departments seeking to migrate away from VMware following Broadcom's acquisition and subsequent licensing changes. Under Ramaswami, Nutanix broadened its market appeal by expanding support for third-party enterprise storage arrays from vendors like Dell, NetApp, and Pure Storage (Everpure), breaking away from its original model of requiring proprietary virtualized storage.

For the broader industry, the transition signals both stability and changing strategic priorities. Nutanix reported 15 percent revenue growth to $772.4 million in its most recent fiscal quarter, but also recorded its slowest customer acquisition growth in several years amid high hardware costs. As the board conducts an executive search for a replacement CEO, Nutanix is preparing for its "third chapter," which will focus heavily on adapting its enterprise platform to host modern artificial intelligence infrastructure and applications while attempting to capture market share from traditional virtualization vendors.

Who's involved: Nutanix, Rajiv Ramaswami, VMware, Dell, NetApp.

## Why the US Army is about to blow up nearly 10,000 pounds of unstable explosives in Iowa

**Category:** Policy / Security  |  **Found via:** The Verge

This story details an emergency hazardous material management decision following legal oversight and judicial intervention. The U.S. Army faced an immediate safety risk from a legacy stockpile of approximately 9,700 pounds of lead azide stored at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown, Iowa. Over time, lead azide—a volatile primary explosive historically used in military munitions—can degrade and crystallize, rendering it extremely sensitive to environmental shifts like ambient temperature changes or slight physical shocks. 

After attempting chemical neutralization methods that proved too dangerous due to external barrel crystallization, military authorities determined that a controlled open detonation was the only viable option to avert an unpredictable, spontaneous explosion. Environmental groups, including the Iowa Environmental Council and the Sierra Club, sought a legal stay in federal court to halt the detonation pending environmental impact assessments. However, a federal appeals court denied the emergency motion, allowing the U.S. Army to proceed with the planned blast.

To mitigate potential lead particulate contamination spreading downwind, the Army implemented containment strategies, including water-filled barrier walls, heavy foam blankets, gel blocks, and protective tarps over deteriorating facility structures. Local health authorities advised residents within the surrounding area to remain indoors with sealed ventilation or voluntarily evacuate under FEMA assistance. Moving forward, environmental agencies and hazardous materials experts will monitor air quality and soil contamination around the site to assess whether further ecological remediation is required.

Who's involved: U.S. Army, Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives (ATF), Iowa Environmental Council, Sierra Club, and the Iowa Department of Health and Human Services.

## Half of NASA's aging Mars fleet knocked offline by technical issues

**Category:** Industry News  |  **Found via:** Ars Technica

NASA is currently facing significant technical hurdles with its Mars exploration program as two of its four active hardware assets at the red planet have temporarily halted science operations. The Perseverance rover encountered issues with both of its ultra-high frequency (UHF) radios—used to transmit high-rate data to orbiting satellites—forcing engineers to temporarily rely on a much slower, direct-to-Earth X-band connection while investigating. Meanwhile, the Mars Odyssey orbiter, launched in 2001, experienced a failure in one of its reaction wheels. Because spacecraft require at least three functioning reaction wheels to maintain proper orientation in space without burning chemical fuel, Odyssey must now rely on its limited hydrazine thruster reserves until engineers can recover the malfunctioning wheel. 

These concurrent failures highlight a broader systemic vulnerability in space exploration: aging infrastructure. Many of NASA's most critical deep-space assets have far outlived their intended mission lifespans without immediate replacements in place. To mitigate these infrastructure bottlenecks, NASA has initiated plans to modernise its orbital relay network, including a contract with Blue Origin to build dedicated telecommunications orbiters. However, legal challenges from commercial competitors have delayed these deployment efforts. Until next-generation hardware reaches Martian orbit, ongoing scientific research remains heavily dependent on aging platforms susceptible to hardware degradation.

Who's involved: NASA, Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL), Perseverance rover, Mars Odyssey orbiter, Blue Origin, and Rocket Lab.

## Microsoft's historic cloud outage exposed an engineering “fog of war”

**Category:** Industry News  |  **Found via:** Ars Technica

Following a massive global outage in November that disrupted critical cloud services including Exchange Online, Teams, and Outlook, Microsoft initiated an internal 'Code Red' review to overhaul its engineering and incident response infrastructure. The technical root cause involved a cascading failure: an undetected Windows scalability defect introduced months earlier caused severe lock contention in gateway processes. When routine maintenance took a portion of gateway server capacity offline, automatic traffic failovers pushed the remaining infrastructure past its operational limits.

Beyond the initial technical flaw, internal memos revealed severe operational friction during the response effort, characterized by executives as an engineering 'fog of war.' Over 11,000 diagnostic messages were fragmented across dozens of separate chat channels, preventing responders from forming a unified view of the incident and extending recovery times significantly beyond target windows. To solve these structural communication and deployment issues, Microsoft is implementing new workstreams focused on automated load shedding, geographic traffic partitioning, and integrating diagnostic AI tools to streamline operational telemetry during crises.

Who's involved: Microsoft, Microsoft 365 Core team, Jason Henderson, Amanda Silver, and Charles Lamanna.
