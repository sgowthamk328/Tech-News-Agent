## Google's Gemini 4 Carbon looks like a diamond in the rough

**Category:** Technical Release  |  **Found via:** The Register

Google has announced Gemini 4 Carbon, a new flagship artificial intelligence model designed to tackle complex, long-running agentic workloads and advanced software security. In modern AI architecture, models are increasingly specialized not merely to generate text or converse, but to operate autonomously as 'agents'—systems capable of executing multi-step tasks, evaluating code environments, and self-correcting over extended operational periods. Gemini 4 Carbon improves upon its predecessor, Argon, by significantly boosting performance on high-stakes software engineering tasks, specifically vulnerability detection and secure code synthesis. 

The core problem Carbon addresses is the chronic vulnerability and unreliability of automated code generation. Standard foundational language models frequently reproduce insecure coding patterns found in their raw training data, introducing subtle security flaws into production environments. According to Google DeepMind, Carbon doubles the success rate of generating secure code compared to Argon and demonstrates an order-of-magnitude improvement over competing frontier models when generating software patches, such as those evaluated by Google's Chrome security team. To achieve this, the underlying system is optimized to assess security benchmarks like SEC-bench Pro, ExploitGym, and CWE-bench, which evaluate a model's practical capability to identify and mitigate known types of software flaws (Common Weakness Enumerations). 

By matching the baseline pricing of Argon during its launch window before rising to meet competitors like Anthropic's Claude Opus, Google is deploying Carbon as an enterprise-grade agent. It offers a specialized mechanism for automated cybersecurity defense, vulnerability triage, and autonomous patching, directly targeting developer operations where security assurance has traditionally been a bottleneck.

Who's involved: Google, Google DeepMind, Koray Kavukcuoglu (Google DeepMind SVP), Sundar Pichai (Alphabet CEO), and competing AI labs Anthropic and OpenAI.

## Boffin's software found a possible dwarf planet - a result so rare he thought it must be a bug

**Category:** Research  |  **Found via:** The Register

Astronomers analyzing data from the newly active Vera C. Rubin Observatory have identified a strong candidate for our solar system's sixth officially recognized dwarf planet, designated as 2026 ED138. Located at an average orbital distance of roughly 46 astronomical units (AU)—placing it further out than Pluto, which averages 40 AU—the celestial body is estimated to be between 600 and 1,500 kilometers in diameter. Depending on its final measurements, it could rank as either the smallest dwarf planet discovered to date or sit comfortably alongside mid-sized bodies such as Makemake.

The discovery is novel due to the computational method used to extract it from background noise. For decades, trans-Neptunian objects have been extraordinarily difficult to discover when their orbital trajectories pass across the galactic plane of the Milky Way, where the dense concentration of background stars obscures faint, moving foreground objects. Researchers developed automated tracking algorithms specifically designed to ingest billions of faint, moving-point detections across time, cross-correlate them, and determine which points belong to a single, consistent orbital path. This computational filter successfully flagged 2026 ED138 from historical and pre-operational survey data despite heavy optical clutter.

This finding demonstrates that next-generation wide-field survey telescopes paired with high-throughput link-matching software can effectively catalog the hidden outer fringes of our solar system. Looking ahead, this discovery enables astronomers to map the population dynamics of the Kuiper Belt with far greater fidelity, test theories regarding planetary formation in the primordial solar system, and refine orbital mechanics models to locate additional hidden bodies residing in previously untracked blind spots.

Who's involved: Matthew Holman and Kevin Napier (Center for Astrophysics | Harvard & Smithsonian), the Vera C. Rubin Observatory, the National Science Foundation, the US Department of Energy, and the Minor Planet Center.

## Tape is so not dead that Fujifilm will increase production capacity 5x

**Category:** Industry News  |  **Found via:** The Register

Magnetic tape storage has long been perceived by the general public as a relic of early computing, yet it remains one of the foundational backbones of enterprise and cloud infrastructure. Unlike solid-state drives or spinning hard disks, which are engineered for fast, random access to active data, magnetic tape excels at high-capacity 'cold' storage. Cold storage refers to vast troves of information that must be retained indefinitely for compliance, security, or future model training, but does not need to be retrieved in real time. Because tape cartridges require zero electrical power while sitting idle on a shelf or inside automated robotic libraries, they offer drastically lower operating expenses, reduced carbon footprints, and natural air-gapped protection against ransomware attacks.

Fujifilm’s decision to commit ¥10 billion ($64 million) to multiply its Linear Tape-Open (LTO) manufacturing capacity fivefold at its Odawara facility underscores the massive data pressures introduced by modern artificial intelligence. Generative AI development relies on ingesting petabytes to exabytes of raw text, audio, and high-resolution video datasets, as well as preserving checkpoint states and inference logs over multiple years. While hard drives and flash storage are essential for active AI compute nodes, the physical cost and power limitations of datacenters make storing inactive training archives entirely on powered media economically unsustainable.

By scaling production of tenth-generation LTO tapes—which deliver native capacities between 30TB and 40TB per cartridge—Fujifilm is preparing the storage industry for the structural data boom ahead. The broader industry roadmap anticipates sustained technological improvements through generation 14, targeting capacities up to 365TB per cartridge. This massive manufacturing expansion signals to hyperscalers, research institutions, and defense operators that physical tape remains an indispensable, highly economical pillar of enterprise data preservation amidst explosive machine learning workloads.

Who's involved: Fujifilm, the LTO Program consortium (consisting of HPE, IBM, and Quantum Corp).

## Anduril now says its BlueChip laptop will meet Buy American standards at launch

**Category:** Policy / Security  |  **Found via:** The Verge

Defense contractor Anduril has officially shifted its manufacturing targets for its upcoming 'BlueChip' laptop, committing that at least 65 percent of the computer's components will be made domestically within the United States by the time it reaches deployment in 2027. This specific figure is directly tied to the Buy American Act and federal procurement regulations, which require goods procured by government and defense agencies to meet stringent domestic-origin cost thresholds. Historically, modern consumer and enterprise computing hardware has relied almost entirely on complex, interconnected global supply chains centered in East Asia, leaving the United States without an established domestic laptop manufacturing ecosystem.

This target adjustment directly impacts federal defense agencies, military procurement officers, and domestic hardware suppliers. In modern military and intelligence applications, computing hardware poses severe national security and cybersecurity risks. Hardware supply chain tampering, unauthorized firmware backdoors, and counterfeit components can compromise mission-critical defense operations. By partnering with semiconductor designer Qualcomm to assemble military-grade systems on domestic soil and source over half their constituent parts internally, Anduril aims to qualify for specialized military contracts that prohibit standard commercially imported electronics.

Moving forward, Anduril and Qualcomm must now translate broad onshoring pledges into verified, certified component manufacturing across American sub-tier vendors. Sourcing domestic printed circuit boards, specialized chassis materials, microcontrollers, and displays within three years presents substantial logistical hurdles given the existing domestic manufacturing gap. Overcoming these barriers will test whether defense hardware can break away from Asian electronics assembly lines to build a resilient, fully audited sovereign supply chain for high-security applications.

Who's involved: Anduril (founded by Palmer Luckey, with communications VP Shannon Prior) and Qualcomm (led by president and CEO Cristiano Amon).

**Source:** https://www.theverge.com/policy/1021554/anduril-bluechip-65-percent-domestic-buy-american

*The commitment to meet the 65 percent domestic threshold is confirmed by corporate statements, but actual execution and component sourcing feasibility remain unverified claims pending the 2027 launch.*

## Upwind raises $400M after walking away from Datadog acquisition offer

**Category:** Funding / M&A  |  **Found via:** TechCrunch

To understand the business dynamic between Upwind and Datadog, it is helpful to look at how rapidly growing software companies are valued and acquired. Upwind operates in cloud runtime security, a critical subfield of cybersecurity that monitors software applications while they are actively running to detect and stop live threats. Because artificial intelligence is driving both new cloud deployment and sophisticated digital attacks, enterprise demand for real-time cloud security has surged.

Datadog, a major publicly traded provider of observability software—tools that help engineers monitor system health and performance—attempted to buy Upwind for $3.8 billion. In enterprise software acquisitions, buyers typically offer a premium price above a startup's current private valuation to convince founders and investors to sell. However, Datadog's offer matched the $3.8 billion valuation that venture capital investors were already willing to set for Upwind's Series C funding round. Realizing they could secure $400 million in fresh capital while retaining complete control over their business, Upwind's leadership chose to walk away from Datadog and remain independent.

This decision relies heavily on valuation multiples, which compare a company's total estimated value to its Annualized Recurring Revenue (ARR). Upwind is projecting approximately $90 million in ARR by the end of the year, meaning a $3.8 billion valuation assigns it a revenue multiple of roughly 42x. While that is a steep price tag, high-growth AI security startups have recently commanded even higher multiples. By rejecting an acquisition at this price point, Upwind is betting that its rapid quarterly growth—adding $20 million to $30 million in ARR per quarter—will push its valuation significantly higher in the future before an eventual public offering or sale.

This transaction signals that the market for AI-driven cybersecurity remains extremely aggressive. Investors view security as one of the few software sectors where enterprises are willing to spend heavily right now, giving promising startups enough leverage to reject billion-dollar buyout offers from established industry giants.

Who's involved: Upwind (cloud security startup), Datadog (observability software vendor), Bessemer Venture Partners (lead Series C investor), and Amiram Shachar (Upwind founder and CEO).

**Source:** https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/28/upwind-raises-400m-after-walking-away-from-datadog-acquisition-offer/

*While the $400 million Series C round led by Bessemer Venture Partners is confirmed, details regarding Datadog's buyout offer and Upwind's specific revenue figures rely on unnamed sources familiar with the matter.*

## ULA's Vulcan rocket is set to return to flight on Friday the 13th

**Category:** Industry News  |  **Found via:** Ars Technica

United Launch Alliance (ULA) is preparing to return its flagship Vulcan rocket to service following a nine-month grounding caused by solid rocket booster anomalies. For newcomers to the aerospace sector, launch service providers rely on rigorous reliability metrics to satisfy both high-value commercial customers and government defense agencies. Vulcan is central to ULA's future as it transitions away from older Atlas and Delta rocket families, but booster nozzle failures during two of its first four flights severely undermined client confidence. In response, the U.S. Space Force—ULA's most critical customer—swapped six high-priority military payloads over to SpaceX Falcon vehicles to avoid national security delays, highlighting the intense competitive pressure in the launch industry.

The upcoming flight marks a crucial inflection point. The mission will deploy 40 satellites for Amazon Leo, a massive low-Earth orbit broadband project competing directly with SpaceX's Starlink. Because the military requires proof of stability before entrusting ULA with defense assets again, commercial flights like Amazon's are effectively serving as proving grounds. Demonstrating reliability is urgent: ULA holds a contract for 38 Amazon launches, while also being designated for roughly half of the Pentagon's primary national security space launches through the early 2030s. Moreover, the mission debuts a full configuration of six strap-on boosters, an elongated payload fairing, and an updated upper stage, alongside redesigned nozzles manufactured by Northrop Grumman.

If the return flight succeeds, ULA can begin clearing its immense launch backlog and restore its stature as a dependable counterweight to SpaceX's near-monopoly on domestic heavy orbital transport. A failure, conversely, would risk further payload migrations to SpaceX and jeopardize the Department of Defense's long-standing strategy of maintaining dual-source access to orbit.

Who's involved: United Launch Alliance (a Boeing and Lockheed Martin joint venture), Northrop Grumman, Amazon Leo, and the U.S. Space Force.

## Apple’s touchscreen MacBook Pro pushes laptop pricing to a new extreme

**Category:** Technical Release  |  **Found via:** TechCrunch

Apple has officially expanded its personal computer hardware portfolio by introducing its first-ever touchscreen MacBook Pro, alongside an ultra-premium tier of internal specifications. For decades, Apple famously resisted bringing direct touch input to macOS laptops, arguing that vertical touchscreens caused arm fatigue and that touch interactions belonged exclusively on iPadOS. Early compromises like the keyboard-mounted Touch Bar were eventually phased out. This release marks an architectural and philosophy shift, bringing full-display touch capabilities to the company's flagship professional laptop lineup.

Under the hood, the release addresses the computational and storage bottlenecks demanded by modern on-device machine learning workflows, large-scale media production, and complex enterprise development. The top-tier machines incorporate Apple's M5 Max processor, featuring an 18-core central processing unit (CPU) and a 40-core graphics processing unit (GPU). To alleviate the massive data-transfer hurdles present in generative AI model tuning and raw multi-stream 8K video processing, Apple has expanded system bandwidth by supporting up to 128 gigabytes of unified memory and an unprecedented internal solid-state drive (SSD) storage capacity of up to 16 terabytes. 

However, solving these extreme computing constraints pushes the platform's pricing architecture into historic territory. While entry-level configurations begin at $2,899, a maxed-out 16-inch model scales to $14,549—an increase driven largely by high memory and massive NAND flash storage costs. By integrating hardware touch sensors into its professional display stack while simultaneously raising the performance ceiling, Apple solves the problem of high-demand local AI and media computation, while also redefining consumer and workstation price thresholds in the high-end personal computing market.

Who's involved: Apple and its MacBook Pro hardware product line.

## As US ties fray, Europe plans first undersea cable link to Greenland

**Category:** Policy / Security  |  **Found via:** Ars Technica

Undersea fiber-optic cables form the invisible foundation of the global internet, carrying over 95 percent of all intercontinental data traffic. Historically, telecommunications consortiums planned these subsea cable routes almost entirely around cost efficiency and low latency, giving little consideration to geopolitical tensions. However, rising global friction and vulnerabilities along maritime chokepoints—such as the Red Sea, where ongoing military strikes disrupt maintenance—are forcing a significant shift in infrastructure planning. In response, the European Union has published a comprehensive Arctic strategy outlining plans for the Pan-Arctic Cable, the first subsea route directly linking mainland Europe to Greenland, with potential future extensions to Canada, Japan, and South Korea that completely bypass the United States.

This policy pivot directly affects international internet governance, national security posture, and sovereign telecommunications resilience across Europe, North America, and East Asia. Fraying diplomatic relations between Europe and the United States, alongside repeated security provocations—such as foreign vessels dragging anchors over Baltic communications links and Russian submarines rehearsing Arctic cable disruptions—have prompted European officials to prioritize digital sovereignty. By anchoring infrastructure in Greenland, Europe secures an independent corridor while avoiding traditional geopolitical chokepoints and adversarial exclusive economic zones. Moving forward, the European Union is establishing an Arctic Cable Hub to monitor, guard, and service North Atlantic lines, while investing in smart sensor cables that can issue early warnings against sabotage. Successfully executing the project, however, will require navigating treacherous Arctic waters and mobilizing an expanded fleet of specialized icebreaker vessels to deploy and maintain high-capacity fiber lines under extreme conditions.

Who's involved: The European Union, the European Commission, the Danish and Greenlandic governments, Bulk Infrastructure Group AS, and researcher Elisabeth Braw of the Atlantic Council.

## Peptide peddlers cheer as RFK Jr. unilaterally grants access, overruling FDA

**Category:** Policy / Security  |  **Found via:** Ars Technica

In United States drug regulation, the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) typically oversees whether pharmaceuticals demonstrate safety and efficacy before they can be legally marketed or manufactured. Specialized compounders—pharmacies that formulate custom medications or prepare bulk ingredients—are generally restricted from mass-producing substances unless specifically vetted and listed under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act. In 2023, the FDA removed six popular peptide substances (BPC-157, Epitalon, KPV, MOTS-c, Semax, and TB-500) from the permissible bulk compounding list due to potential health hazards and an absence of human clinical data. However, Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. has now issued an executive "enforcement discretion" directive instructing federal regulators not to pursue legal action against compounding pharmacies that synthesize and sell these specific peptides, effectively overriding agency scientists without formally reversing the underlying FDA classification.

This administrative shift immediately affects compounding pharmacies, commercial wellness clinics, conventional pharmaceutical manufacturers, and consumers. Proponents celebrate the rule as a triumph of personal medical freedom, arguing that regulated domestic compounding offers a safer alternative to unmonitored gray-market imports. Conversely, public health experts, medical groups, and consumer advocates caution that the decision exposes patients to unverified and potentially hazardous treatments while degrading established safety standards. Commercial pharmaceutical developers also lose incentives to invest in rigorous clinical trials when unapproved substances can circulate legally without pre-market clearance. Moving forward, the policy is likely to provoke legal challenges regarding administrative authority and conflicts of interest, particularly given Kennedy's prior restructuring of the relevant advisory committee, while healthcare providers brace for an increase in unverified peptide marketing across the country.

Who's involved: Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., the Food and Drug Administration (FDA), compounding pharmacies, the American Academy of Peptide Medicine, and the Center for Science in the Public Interest.
