For example, OpenAI released a infrastructure blueprint committing to abandon mandatory non-disclosure agreements with local governments while attempting to rebrand industrial facilities as 'compute campuses.' 

Moving forward, the industry faces an era where local permitting and state policy will introduce significant friction to construction timelines. With potential legislative gridlock in Washington, state capitols and municipal zoning boards will serve as the primary venues determining how and where data infrastructure can be built. Consequently, technology companies will need to engage in more transparent community negotiation, navigate regional moratoriums, or explore development outside conventional hubs to satisfy their computational demands.

## The UV-tracking Gem made me rethink how sunlight affects my health, not just my appearance

**Category:** Technical Release  |  **Found via:** The Verge

The90 Gem is a wearable health tracker designed as a necklace pendant that solves the problem of inaccurate, ambient-only ultraviolet (UV) radiation monitoring. Most consumer weather apps provide general UV index forecasts based on regional meteorological data. However, localized ambient forecasts fail to account for specific micro-environments, shade penetration, clothing coverage, or reflective radiation from nearby surfaces. Furthermore, standard forecasts blend overall radiation into a single number rather than distinguishing between distinct wavelengths that affect human biology differently.

The Gem tackles this through dedicated, miniature onboard sensors that independently measure UVA and UVB wavelengths in real time. UVA rays have longer wavelengths that penetrate deep into the skin's dermis, contributing primarily to cellular damage and premature aging, while UVB rays have shorter wavelengths responsible for sunburns and vitamin D synthesis. The Gem requires an active Bluetooth link to a smartphone application running in the background to offload, log, and process this sensor data. 

Inside the companion software, incoming radiometric readings are contextualized against an algorithmic user profile incorporating the wearer's Fitzpatrick skin type, sun sensitivities, applied sunscreen, and clothing choices. This architecture allows the platform to calculate personalized real-time metrics rather than static estimates, including a live sunburn countdown, an estimate of natural vitamin D production, and cumulative UVA exposure scores. By translating raw wavelength exposure into tailored physiological feedback, the device provides actionable environmental health data without relying on bulky form factors or invasive medical monitors.

Who's involved: The90 (hardware manufacturer) and its wearable product, the Gem.

## Fireworks says you’re wasting money on AI unless you’re using all the models

**Category:** Industry News  |  **Found via:** The Register

As enterprises rush to adopt generative artificial intelligence, they face an immense financial challenge: running state-of-the-art AI models requires vast amounts of specialized hardware, primarily graphics processing units (GPUs). Purchasing or reserving dedicated GPUs can quickly lead to millions of dollars in capital expenditure, which becomes deeply inefficient whenever that hardware sits idle between tasks. Fireworks AI, an inference platform provider, argues that relying on a single dominant AI model or purchasing dedicated on-premises infrastructure is an economically flawed strategy. Instead, the company advocates for managed inference and multi-model ensembling—a setup where user queries are dynamically inspected and routed across a diverse portfolio of AI models based on the specific difficulty, domain, and cost of each task.

This shift in strategy highlights a maturing AI market where raw benchmark performance is no longer the sole metric of success; cost-per-task, latency, and hardware utilization now dominate operational decisions. Large proprietary models excel at complex reasoning but are excessively expensive for routine chores like basic data extraction or classification, which smaller open-source models can handle just as well for a fraction of the cost. Furthermore, inference providers leverage aggressive prompt caching to avoid recomputing identical queries—a metric that major frontier model providers often keep opaque. Fireworks claims that combining intelligent routing with caching can cut an enterprise's AI inference expenses by at least half compared to uniform model usage.

The broader implication for the technology sector is a shift away from standard "monolithic" vendor lock-in. Instead of tying products entirely to a single cloud provider or frontier model developer like OpenAI or Anthropic, organizations are increasingly turning to intermediate routing fabrics. As model architectures diversify and open-source models close the gap with commercial alternatives, the competitive advantage in enterprise AI is tilting toward infrastructure platforms that master system topology, low-level numerical efficiency, and intelligent workload distribution.

Who's involved: Fireworks AI (featuring VP Rob Ferguson), competing inference providers Together AI, Baseten, and DeepInfra, alongside foundation model providers Anthropic and Microsoft.

## The Bantu expansion changed Africa. Scientists are still tracing how.

**Category:** Research  |  **Found via:** Ars Technica

For decades, scholars have sought to explain how sub-Saharan Africa came to be dominated by hundreds of closely related languages and shared technological practices, such as advanced iron smelting and farming. This historical transformation stems from the Bantu expansion, a massive demographic event that began thousands of years ago in western Africa. A synthesis of research across linguistics, archaeology, and population genetics provides fresh clarity on the path, mechanisms, and social dynamics of this continental movement, challenging simpler, linear models of how human populations disperse and exchange technology.

The findings show that rather than spreading purely via ideas transmitted between stationary communities, the expansion was propelled by substantial physical migrations of people. Genetic sequencing demonstrates that Bantu populations across central, eastern, and southern Africa share recent common ancestors, with genetic diversity decreasing the further populations traveled from their homeland in the Nigeria-Cameroon borderlands. Importantly, recent linguistic and genetic modeling refutes the idea that early migrants immediately split into two isolated paths around the Congo rainforest. Instead, evidence shows that ancestral groups traversed the dense rainforest together, undergoing extensive linguistic and cultural diversification before splitting into distinct eastward and southward routes once they emerged along the southern rim.

Furthermore, genomic analysis of ancient and contemporary lineages reveals complex interactions with indigenous hunter-gatherers, including Khoisan and rainforest groups. Genetic markers show asymmetric intermixing—disproportionately involving Bantu men and indigenous women—alongside technological and linguistic exchanges, such as the adoption of characteristic click sounds into southern Bantu tongues. Novel archaeometallurgical findings near Great Zimbabwe also demonstrate that Bantu societies independently refined high-heat, self-drafting iron furnaces, utilizing organic additives like bone to optimize chemical reactions.

These discoveries enable researchers to build high-resolution, multi-layered models of human demographic history. They provide a blueprint for extracting ancient DNA in tropical regions and call for revised computational linguistic methods that account for cross-community language contact rather than isolated evolutionary trees.

Who's involved: Archaeometallurgist Ezekiel Mtetwa (Field Museum), geneticists Carina Schlebusch (Uppsala University), Sandra Beleza (University of Leicester), and Ananyo Choudhury (University of the Witwatersrand), alongside linguists Koen Bostoen (Ghent University) and Igor Yanovich (University of Vienna).

**Source:** https://arstechnica.com/science/2026/11/the-migration-that-transformed-africas-languages-genes-and-cultures/

*While the broad genetic trajectory and demographic expansion are confirmed by peer-reviewed genomic datasets, hypotheses regarding specific social dynamics (such as asymmetric marriage practices or the exact origin point of iron-smelting invention) remain ongoing academic interpretations.*

## British soldier ignored order to pull his SIM card, then got blackmailed

**Category:** Policy / Security  |  **Found via:** Ars Technica

This case highlights the strict intersection of military cybersecurity policy and personal operational discipline during foreign deployments. During military exercises near sensitive geopolitical borders—such as NATO maneuvers in Eastern Europe—cellular networks are heavily monitored by foreign signals intelligence (SIGINT) agencies. Active SIM cards continuously broadcast unique identifiers to local cellular towers, allowing hostile actors to track troop movements, pinpoint operational bases, intercept communications, and identify high-value targets for digital compromise. To mitigate these risks, military commands mandate strict operational security (OpSec) protocols: personnel must remove SIM cards, disable hardware location services, and route any wireless internet traffic through approved virtual private networks (VPNs).

In this incident, British soldier Daniel Barton-Kennedy defied explicit pre-deployment briefings and on-the-ground reminders to remove his personal SIM card while deployed to Estonia, less than 100 miles from the Russian border. The breach directly compromised the soldier: an unknown threat actor breached his smartphone, exfiltrated private photographs, matched his identity to his social media accounts, and attempted extortion. Instead of maintaining radio and electronic silence, the soldier's defiance created an immediate attack vector that threatened not only his personal welfare but also organizational intelligence security.

The consequences demonstrate that operational security rules are treated with severe legal weight rather than administrative leniency. Barton-Kennedy was court-martialed for disobeying a lawful order, demoted in rank, and sentenced to 10 months of detention—a sentence upheld upon appeal. Moving forward, the incident reinforces that defense organizations will enforce zero-tolerance judicial penalties against personnel who fail to adhere to device sanitation and isolation mandates. Military branches globally are likely to intensify enforcement, utilizing automated spectrum monitoring or confiscating personal electronic devices entirely during sensitive border deployments to eliminate human error.

Who's involved: British Army, Corporal Daniel Barton-Kennedy, 1st Battalion of the Duke of Lancaster Regiment, and an unidentified extortionist.

## NHS breach helpline left staff carrying a 'significant emotional burden'

**Category:** Policy / Security  |  **Found via:** The Register

This incident exposes the administrative, technical, and human operational failures that can arise from mishandling data disclosure laws like the Freedom of Information Act (FOIA). In public administration, FOIA requests demand transparency while strictly requiring the redaction of personally identifiable and sensitive information. Technically, complex data tools like Microsoft Excel can retain hidden cached data within pivot tables even if the underlying table records seem invisible or deleted from active rows. If released improperly, a public-facing file can allow outside users to double-click pivot table aggregations and automatically regenerate the entire raw, sensitive dataset.

A published review revealed that the Royal Cornwall Hospitals NHS Trust published an Excel file containing underlying sickness absence records for 8,003 individuals. The data was exposed publicly for 33 months because non-expert internal staff handled the request, skipped procedural sanitation steps—such as using Excel's Document Inspector—and failed to flatten the spreadsheet into a non-functional format like CSV or PDF. Beyond the technical leak, the policy fallout heavily impacted the internal workforce: clinical governance staff were conscripted on 24 hours' notice to staff a crisis support helpline with almost no training, confronting distressed colleagues and domestic abuse safeguarding issues over an 11-week period instead of the planned two weeks.

Following the investigation, the trust revamped its governance framework to prevent future data breaches. Excel workbooks submitted for public disclosures now mandate password protection, explicit CSV export rules to strip embedded metadata and caches, and a multi-tiered approval hierarchy requiring sign-off from a senior information governance officer before public distribution. Although the Information Commissioner's Office closed its investigation following these remedial measures, healthcare institutions face mounting scrutiny to implement hardened technical safeguards and standardized human-support frameworks during incident responses.

Who's involved: Royal Cornwall Hospitals NHS Trust and the UK Information Commissioner's Office (ICO).
