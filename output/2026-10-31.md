## Pile-ins, payouts, and pacing pressure: the investors behind the VCs open up

**Category:** Industry News  |  **Found via:** TechCrunch

This story offers a rare look into the perspectives of limited partners (LPs)—the institutional investors who fund venture capital firms. In venture capital, LPs traditionally diversify their investments across numerous funds and sectors to hedge against risk. However, the unprecedented rise of generative AI companies like OpenAI and Anthropic has drastically altered this playbook, resulting in extreme concentration as venture firms pile into the exact same high-valuation foundation model companies.

While LPs are currently pleased with high paper returns, the market dynamics raise significant strategic concerns. Venture funds are deploying capital at an accelerated pace, returning to market to raise new funds in as little as 12 to 18 months rather than the standard two-to-three-year cycle. This rapid fund deployment strains LP liquidity, as capital is called faster than older investments can generate cash distributions. To manage these risks, the market is turning heavily toward secondary liquidity mechanisms—such as selling fund stakes or executing employee tender offers—to return capital before formal public offerings occur.

These dynamics signal an evolving venture ecosystem where extreme valuation growth is forcing LPs to re-evaluate traditional fund cadence, portfolio concentration rules, and liquidity strategies. Emerging managers face heightened scrutiny to prove distinct sourcing advantages, while established firms navigate the operational pressure of deploying massive funds in record time.

**Category:** Funding / M&A  |  **Found via:** TechCrunch

To help Nscale secure loan approvals from lenders, Nvidia is considering providing credit support known as a residual value guarantee, essentially acting as a corporate co-signer on Nscale's debt.

This arrangement reflects a growing trend in the AI hardware market where sovereign funds, neoclouds, and specialized data center providers rely on heavy leverage to finance the physical buildout of high-density compute facilities housing Nvidia's advanced chips, such as the Vera Rubin architecture.

This transaction signals a key market shift: traditional lenders are becoming increasingly cautious about funding speculative or highly leveraged AI infrastructure projects without credit enhancements from mega-cap tech partners. By offering backstop guarantees, Nvidia ensures continuous market demand and timely deployment for its high-margin microprocessors while shielding compute providers from tight credit markets.

## Once again, NASA is asking for ideas to rescue Hubble from certain death

**Category:** Industry News  |  **Found via:** Ars Technica

The Hubble Space Telescope has spent over three decades providing groundbreaking astronomical data, but atmospheric drag is gradually pulling it down toward Earth. Left unassisted, orbital mechanics dictate that the telescope will burn up during uncontrolled atmospheric reentry by the early-to-mid 2030s. To prevent this outcome, NASA has issued a formal Request For Information (RFI) seeking commercial solutions to capture and reboost Hubble to a higher altitude—at least 373 miles (600 km)—or facilitate a safe, controlled disposal.

This decision highlights a major shift in how national space agencies approach orbital infrastructure. Historically, servicing missions to government satellites were multi-billion-dollar operations executed by NASA using specialized hardware like the Space Shuttle. However, since the retirement of the Shuttle fleet and the growth of commercial space enterprises, a private satellite servicing industry has rapidly emerged. Companies now utilize robotic spacecraft equipped with autonomous rendezvous capabilities, docking mechanisms, and micro-propulsion to extend the lifespans of commercial and defense satellites. NASA's call for proposals reflects a strategic effort to leverage these lower-cost, private-sector innovations rather than designing dedicated agency-funded missions.

Reboosting Hubble by roughly 80 miles would extend its operational lifespan by at least 25 years. While newer space observatories operate primarily in the infrared spectrum, Hubble provides critical observations in visible and ultraviolet light. Preserving its capabilities bridges an important scientific gap until future flagship space telescopes launch decades from now. The broader industry implications suggest that satellite servicing is transitioning from experimental demonstrations into a standard commercial service model, changing how high-value space assets are maintained over long lifespans.

Who's involved: NASA, SpaceX, Northrop Grumman, Astroscale, Momentus, Starfish Space, and Katalyst Space Technologies.

## Flesh-eating screwworms spread to third state and are attacking pets

**Category:** Industry News  |  **Found via:** Ars Technica

The New World Screwworm (NWS) is a parasitic fly species whose larvae feed exclusively on the living tissue of warm-blooded animals. Unlike common blowflies that target decaying organic matter, adult screwworm flies lay eggs inside open wounds or natural body orifices of living hosts. Once hatched, the larvae use specialized sharp mouth hooks to burrow deep into muscle and tissue, creating expanding, severe lesions that can quickly cause fatal infections or systemic shock if untreated. 

Historically, the United States successfully eradicated domestic screwworm populations during the mid-twentieth century through a large-scale biological control initiative known as the Sterile Insect Technique. By releasing millions of factory-reared, radiation-sterilized male flies into affected regions, wild female flies mated without producing viable offspring, leading to population collapse. For decades, a permanent biological barrier was maintained along the narrow Darién Gap between Panama and Colombia to prevent northern re-invasion from endemic South American populations. However, that containment barrier failed in 2022, allowing screwworm populations to migrate north through Central America and Mexico into southern U.S. border regions.

The recent expansion of verified screwworm infestations into Arizona—following cases in Texas and New Mexico—signals a serious operational challenge for domestic agriculture, veterinary medicine, and wildlife management. While primary impacts threaten livestock productivity and ranching economics, recent cases confirm that domestic pets like dogs and cats are increasingly falling victim to localized infestations in suburban and rural areas. Veterinary authorities are responding by escalating active surveillance, urging immediate case reporting, and establishing regional monitoring networks to contain fly movement before broader commercial herds suffer severe losses.

Who's involved: U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA), Arizona Department of Agriculture, New Mexico Department of Agriculture, and local veterinary clinics.

## Exclusive details about Akai’s MPC Sample Key

**Category:** Technical Release  |  **Found via:** The Verge

This report details leaked information and physical hardware images of Akai's upcoming MPC Sample Key, a dedicated hardware sampler designed with a open-source software ecosystem. Traditionally, standalone sampling hardware—like Akai’s famous Music Production Center (MPC) series—relies heavily on grid pads optimized for percussive audio slicing, or 'drum chopping.' In drum chopping, a producer records a snippet of music, slices it into distinct hit points across dynamic pads, and triggers those slices rhythmically to construct new beats. The MPC Sample Key shifts this architectural paradigm by targeting melodic and harmonic sample manipulation instead.

To achieve this, the device replaces conventional chopping workflows with performance-oriented hardware controls built around a 25-key musical keyboard. Physical design elements include dedicated pitch-bend and modulation wheels, octave transposition buttons, an onboard arpeggiator, a dedicated chord mode, and a performance looper. An arpeggiator automatically sequences triggered notes into repeating melodic patterns, while a chord mode maps complex multi-note structures to individual key presses—tools that are fundamentally tailored for tonal synths and melodic leads rather than rhythmic drum breaks. Furthermore, the unit features contextual rotary encoders, built-in microphones, speakers, and control voltage (CV) outputs, enabling direct integration with analog modular synthesizers.

By committing to open-source software, Akai allows the developer and hardware-modding community to write custom firmware, modify internal DSP algorithms, and create customized performance modes. This solves the problem of rigid, vendor-locked hardware ecosystems, providing electronic musicians with an extensible platform for live performance and portable music creation.

## Sam Altman says it’s better for RAM to be in a data center than on your desk

**Category:** Industry News  |  **Found via:** The Verge

Random Access Memory (RAM) serves as a computer's short-term working memory, storing the active data needed by software to execute tasks quickly. As artificial intelligence models have grown larger, their memory requirements have surged. Running AI models natively on personal devices—known as local inference—requires substantial local RAM. However, the global tech market is currently experiencing a severe shortage of dynamic random-access memory (DRAM), leading to rising prices across consumer electronics like laptops, smartphones, and desktop computers.

In an appearance on the Cheeky Pint podcast, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman addressed this global memory crunch. He argued that from a global resource efficiency standpoint, housing high-capacity RAM inside consumer PCs is inefficient because local computers sit idle most of the day, leaving their memory underutilized. Instead, Altman advocated for routing RAM supply directly into centralized cloud data centers. In cloud environments, server infrastructure operates continuously around the clock, allowing data centers to achieve near-maximum hardware utilization by serving millions of users simultaneously.

This perspective reflects a broader industry debate regarding centralized cloud computing versus decentralized local computing. While privacy advocates and open-source AI developers favor running models directly on personal hardware to keep data local and avoid subscription fees, AI cloud providers depend heavily on massive data center capacity. Major memory chip manufacturers, including Samsung and SK Hynix, have increasingly locked in multi-year supply contracts with hyper-scalers and AI ventures like OpenAI's Stargate project. Because cloud providers are willing to pay premium prices to secure RAM supplies, consumer hardware makers face reduced availability and higher component costs, passing those price hikes onto everyday consumers.

Who's involved: OpenAI, Sam Altman, Samsung, SK Hynix, Apple, and Microsoft.

## Darkbloom, which allowed Mac users to rent out their GPUs for AI inference, warns of low payouts

**Category:** Industry News  |  **Found via:** The Register

Artificial intelligence inference is the process where a trained model receives user prompts and generates output. To complete these tasks, models process data using discrete units called tokens, which roughly correspond to parts of words. Historically, renting computer processing power (GPUs and RAM) to perform inference was expensive, creating an opportunity for distributed networks. Distributed inference platforms aggregate spare processing power from individual consumer devices—such as Apple Mac computers—and sell that computing capacity to AI platforms that route traffic across the network.

Darkbloom, a distributed inference network created by Eigen Labs, allowed individual Mac owners to run background software, process inference requests sent via routing services like OpenRouter, and receive financial payouts based on the volume of tokens processed. However, Darkbloom recently alerted its contributors that provider earnings have dropped significantly and are unlikely to recover soon. The underlying issue is a sharp nationwide drop in AI token prices, driven by efficiency gains in centralized data centers. Advances such as DeepSeek's DeepInfer framework and optimized server clusters enabled data center operators to increase token output while drastically cutting prices, undercutting the cost advantage that crowdsourced networks once possessed.

Furthermore, API aggregators like OpenRouter have shifted their routing algorithms. Rather than choosing processing providers solely based on the lowest price, platforms now prioritize operational speed, low latency, and compute architecture. Standard consumer Macs struggle to match the raw speed of specialized enterprise server racks. In response to these market changes, Darkbloom is adjusting its strategy. The project plans to pivot away from standalone consumer devices toward specialized local hardware clusters, while exploring alternative workloads such as automated continuous integration and software deployment tasks.

Who's involved: Darkbloom, Eigen Labs, OpenRouter, Anthropic, OpenAI, and DeepSeek.
