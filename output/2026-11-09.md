## Linus Torvalds sees signs AI-prompted patch flood has peaked

**Category:** Industry News  |  **Found via:** The Register

This story reports on comments made by Linux creator Linus Torvalds regarding the volume of security vulnerability reports and code patches currently hitting major open-source software projects.

Over the past year, the widespread adoption of specialized AI bug-finding tools—such as Anthropic's Mythos model—has fundamentally changed software maintenance. Historically, discovering security vulnerabilities (known as zero-days) required manual source code inspection or automated testing by specialized security researchers. Large language models and AI vulnerability scanners automated this process at scale, generating massive floods of bug reports across major codebases, including Windows, Oracle databases, and the Linux kernel. This created a severe operational burden for open-source maintainers and software vendors, who had to triage, verify, and write code patches faster than ever to prevent malicious actors from exploiting the newly surfaced bugs.

Torvalds' observation signals a potential operational turning point for the software industry. Citing reports from key maintainers of Extended Berkeley Packet Filter (eBPF)—a crucial Linux subsystem that allows sandboxed programs to run inside the operating system kernel—he noted that inbound security fixes are finally beginning to outpace incoming bug reports. If this trend spreads across the rest of the 40-million-line Linux kernel and software development at large, it implies that automated AI vulnerability scanners may be running out of easy-to-find legacy security flaws. For the tech ecosystem, reaching this equilibrium means developers can shift their focus back to core software features rather than remaining in an unsustainable, continuous cycle of emergency security patching.

Who's involved: Linus Torvalds, Alexei Starovoitov (eBPF co-creator), Linux Kernel Organization, Anthropic, Microsoft, and Oracle.

**Source:** https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/09/linus-torvalds-sees-signs-ai-prompted-patch-flood-has-peaked/5308345

*Linus Torvalds' statements regarding the Linux 7.4 rc2 release and eBPF patch rates are confirmed, but his broader assessment that the industry-wide AI vulnerability flood has peaked remains an optimistic prediction rather than a verified fact.*

## Huawei says it’s made a 100 percent Chinese PC

**Category:** Industry News  |  **Found via:** The Register

China has been pursuing a national policy of replacing foreign computing technology with domestic alternatives, aiming to completely phase out Western hardware and operating systems from government offices and state-owned enterprises. In line with this mandate, Huawei has launched the Qingyun HM650 desktop PC, claiming it to be constructed entirely of domestically manufactured components. The machine features Huawei’s custom Kirin X90 processor—a ten-core, 20-thread CPU—and operates on an enterprise edition of HarmonyOS, the operating system Huawei built to end its reliance on Android and Windows.

This development carries significant implications for the global tech sector. If Chinese domestic hardware can sufficiently meet government and enterprise operational requirements, foreign chipmakers like Intel, AMD, and Qualcomm risk losing access to one of the world’s largest enterprise technology markets. China’s public sector represents massive procurement volume, and being locked out of government contracts deprives Western firms of substantial baseline revenue during a period when global consumer PC demand is already constrained by rising component costs.

However, industry analysts remain cautious about the claim of a completely sovereign supply chain. Manufacturing a modern computer requires hundreds of discrete subcomponents, silicon fabrication steps, and proprietary designs. While the physical assembly and manufacturing of all parts may occur within Chinese borders, several key components might still rely on intellectual property, architecture designs, or foreign-owned manufacturing facilities operating inside China. Regardless of whether absolute hardware independence has been achieved, the launch demonstrates China's rapid progress toward creating an isolated, self-sustaining technological ecosystem, accelerating the technological decoupling between East and West.

Who's involved: Huawei, China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, Intel, AMD, Qualcomm, and Apple.

## Epic Games Launcher is coming to SteamOS

**Category:** Industry News  |  **Found via:** The Verge

The PC gaming ecosystem has historically been fragmented across distinct digital storefronts and operating systems. While Microsoft Windows has long served as the default platform for desktop gaming, Valve's release of the Linux-based SteamOS and its handheld Steam Deck hardware shifted consumer expectations toward portable, open-source gaming. However, running games from competing storefronts—such as the Epic Games Store—on SteamOS has traditionally required third-party workarounds and community-maintained compatibility layers, creating friction for users who own titles across multiple platforms.

Epic Games confirmed that its native Linux launcher preview will officially support SteamOS alongside popular Linux distributions like Fedora, Cachy, and Omarchy. As part of this rollout, Epic is restoring support for the AppImage container format while evaluating alternative Linux package formats such as Debian and Ubuntu binaries. By building first-party support for SteamOS, Epic reduces technical hurdles for gamers who want direct access to their Epic Games library on portable hardware without jumping through installation workarounds.

Despite this progress, significant hurdles remain before Epic’s premier multiplayer titles can operate seamlessly on Valve’s hardware platform. Popular online multiplayer games like Fortnite rely on kernel-level security software—specifically Easy Anti-Cheat (EAC) v2—to prevent cheating. Currently, SteamOS lacks support for the specific features required by EAC v2. Epic stated it is actively collaborating with Valve to solve these underlying anti-cheat technical constraints. If resolved, official launcher support and anti-cheat compatibility would dismantle major barriers between competing game distribution platforms, accelerating broader Linux adoption across mainstream PC gaming.

## Physicists listened to electrical noise to crack a superconductor mystery

**Category:** Research  |  **Found via:** Ars Technica

Superconductors are materials that transmit electrical current with zero energy lost to heat. While conventional superconductors only exhibit this behavior near absolute zero, copper-based compounds known as cuprates operate at significantly higher temperatures achievable with liquid nitrogen. For decades, scientists have debated the cause of the "pseudogap," a phase in cuprates where electrons appear to vanish at specific energy levels prior to full superconductivity. Two competing theories emerged: one suggesting electrons freeze into static structural patterns like charge density waves, and another proposing electrons form bound pairs—known as Cooper pairs—well before they begin flowing together in lockstep.

A team of physicists resolved this long-standing debate by measuring subtle electrical fluctuations known as "shot noise." Shot noise occurs when discrete electrical charges hit a barrier, analogous to the sound of individual raindrops hitting a tin roof. If electrons enter a material as bound pairs rather than single particles, the heavier effective charge produces a noticeably louder electrical crackle. Using a specialized scanning tunneling microscope operating at precise voltage levels and atomic positions, the researchers analyzed the shot noise of current flowing into a cuprate sample called BSCCO.

The experimental measurements revealed that electron pairing begins precisely at the pseudogap energy level, rather than at the lower temperature where superconductivity actually starts. When magnetic fields were applied to suppress pairing, the electrical noise dropped back down to single-electron levels, confirming that pre-formed Cooper pairs were responsible. This finding proves that in high-temperature cuprates, pair formation occurs long before the pairs achieve "phase coherence"—the state where they move together as a single fluid. This insight shifts the focus of high-temperature superconductivity research from getting electrons to pair to overcoming the bottleneck of making those pairs move coherently, giving materials scientists a clearer roadmap for designing room-temperature superconductors.

Who's involved: Milan Allan (Ludwig Maximilian University of Munich), Ingmar Swart (Utrecht University), Science journal.
