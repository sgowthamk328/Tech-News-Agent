Even though the automated script ran smoothly, unstable underlying telecommunication switches triggered constant pager alerts. Furthermore, organizational issues—such as offloading on-call responsibilities to colleagues who neglected the hardware—demonstrate that streamlining code cannot fully eliminate operational sleep deprivation without sound processes.

## Alibaba's Qwen3.8 re-educated with a more Western worldview

**Category:** Technical Release  |  **Found via:** The Register

This story describes a post-training technique known as machine unlearning applied to open-weight artificial intelligence models. Large language models (LLMs) learn patterns from massive text datasets during pre-training. When an open-source model is developed in a specific region, such as China, its underlying parameters retain region-specific alignment, propaganda, and self-censorship regarding sensitive historical or geopolitical topics like Tiananmen Square. While open-weight models permit public downloading and modification, simply prompting them to ignore their training often fails because political biases are deeply encoded within the neural network's weights.

To solve this issue, independent AI labs use targeted post-training supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and machine unlearning algorithms. Researchers feed the base model curated dataset pairs—in this case using an open-weight teacher model called Inkling from Thinking Machines Lab—to overwrite specific behavioral pathways. By presenting 50 million tokens of non-censored, fact-aligned conversational data, the fine-tuning process effectively dampens the neuron activations responsible for generating state-aligned responses. The technical accomplishment demonstrates that state-imposed censorship layers in AI models are surprisingly shallow and can be stripped out without retraining the model from scratch.

This unlearning approach solves the problem of regional compliance lock-in for open-source AI developers. Enterprise users can now leverage high-performing, open-weight model architectures originating from foreign labs while removing geopolitical biases, censorship constraints, or security vulnerabilities before deploying them in Western markets.

Who's involved: Alibaba (Qwen3.8), Nous Research (Arete-27B), Thinking Machines Lab (Inkling model), Hirundo, and Booz Allen Hamilton.

## Whoop launched a social network where every user has a ‘verified heartbeat’

**Category:** Technical Release  |  **Found via:** The Verge

Fitness technology company Whoop has launched a new social networking platform called Whoop Humans, integrated directly within its existing mobile application under the Community tab. The platform addresses a growing problem on modern social networks: the proliferation of automated accounts, AI-generated content, and unverified users. Whoop solves this identity verification challenge by leveraging its hardware sensors as a physical proof-of-presence mechanism—requiring each account to be tied to a active Whoop wearable device measuring real-time biometric telemetry, effectively creating a network where every user has a verified physical heartbeat.

Architecturally, Whoop Humans functions by capturing raw biometric data from the user's wrist-worn device—including heart rate variability (HRV), resting heart rate, sleep tracking, and physical strain scores—and allowing users to layer that data onto visual posts called "Moments." Similar to user interface patterns seen in popular social platforms like Instagram Stories, users can post photo updates accompanied by overlaid metric cards or workout maps detailing route, distance, and pace. When viewing a post, other users can interact by commenting, sending reactions, or expanding shared cards to view deeper metrics.

To address privacy risks inherent in broadcast biometric sharing, the system incorporates granular privacy controls. Users select specifically which metrics (such as sleep duration or recovery scores) are exposed publicly, while activities designated as sensitive remain hidden by default. Furthermore, the architecture includes a "Stealth Mode" toggle, which allows users to temporarily pause data transmission to the social feed without resetting or altering their global privacy settings.

By converting raw physiological data captured via photoplethysmography (optical heart rate monitoring) into verified social content, Whoop provides a closed-loop platform that authenticates human identity through continuous physiological telemetry while enabling fitness-focused social interaction.

## In reversal, American Cancer Society urges routine prostate cancer screening

**Category:** Policy / Security  |  **Found via:** Ars Technica

The American Cancer Society (ACS) has significantly revised its official clinical guidance regarding prostate cancer screening. In a shift away from its 2010 guidelines—which previously favored individual, shared decision-making between patients and physicians without universal mandates—the ACS now explicitly recommends routine prostate-specific antigen (PSA) blood tests for all men within specific age and risk categories.

Under the updated rules, men at average risk are advised to undergo routine PSA testing every two years starting from age 50 through 69. For higher-risk groups, including all Black men and men with a family history of early prostate cancer diagnoses, testing is recommended annually starting at age 45. To mitigate long-standing medical concerns over false positives, unnecessary biopsies, overtreatment, and side effects like incontinence or erectile dysfunction, the ACS introduced clear procedural guardrails: high PSA readings must be retested before taking further clinical action, followed by magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) or validated biomarker tests rather than jumping straight to invasive tissue biopsies. Active surveillance is formally recommended as the primary management strategy for low-risk cases.

This policy directly affects adult men across the United States, primary care clinicians, urologists, and public health practitioners who design preventive health schedules. Looking ahead, primary care practices will adapt patient intake protocols to align with these revised timelines. Meanwhile, federal health oversight bodies, such as the US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF), are actively evaluating their own guidance updates to determine whether national health insurance coverage standards and quality metrics will follow suit.

Who's involved: American Cancer Society (ACS), US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF), Dr. Shanthi Sivendran.

## FAA expands AI advice to manage air traffic congestion across entire US airspace

**Category:** Policy / Security  |  **Found via:** Ars Technica

The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) has announced a major expansion of its artificial intelligence system, known as SMART (System Wide Management and Optimization Tools), deploying it across all 22 air route traffic control centers in the United States. Following an initial test rollout in the National Capital Region surrounding Washington, D.C., the $875 million cloud-based software is now being implemented nationwide to assist in managing air traffic congestion, reducing flight delays, and lowering fuel consumption.

The SMART system aggregates real-time data from multiple operational sources, including weather forecasts, flight trajectories, overall traffic flow patterns, and airport staffing metrics. By processing these complex variables simultaneously, the system predicts traffic bottlenecks and automatically generates recommendations for optimal flight routing, arrival scheduling, and altitude management. In preliminary trials, the FAA reported that SMART successfully rerouted 180 flights, saving an average of 10 minutes and nearly 80 gallons of fuel per flight.

This policy shift directly impacts several key stakeholders in civil aviation. Commercial airlines and passengers stand to benefit from reduced delays and fuel burn, earning explicit endorsements from major carrier leadership. However, the National Air Traffic Controllers Association (NATCA), which represents over 15,000 air traffic controllers, expressed serious concerns, stating that the union was completely excluded from the design, testing, and implementation phases of the system.

To mitigate safety risks, the FAA emphasized that SMART serves purely in an advisory capacity and cannot directly control aircraft or replace human decision-making. Recommendations generated by the cloud platform are first evaluated and validated by a dedicated team of human FAA specialists in Washington, D.C., before being forwarded to local control facilities. Furthermore, frontline air traffic controllers retain full operational discretion to accept or reject the AI's suggestions based on their localized judgment. Moving forward, the agency plans to monitor nationwide performance metrics while navigating ongoing workforce relations and union scrutiny regarding the technology's integration into daily tower operations.

Who's involved: Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), National Air Traffic Controllers Association (NATCA), Delta Air Lines, and American Airlines.

## Muse can now place calls to US businesses — if you pay

**Category:** Technical Release  |  **Found via:** TechCrunch

Meta has officially rolled out a phone-calling feature for its consumer artificial intelligence agent, Muse, allowing the assistant to place outbound phone calls to U.S. businesses on behalf of users. Initially trialed with a select group of early testers, the capability is now generally available to paid subscribers on plans starting at $20 per month for 500 million tokens up to $100 per month for heavy usage.

The system addresses a pervasive friction point in everyday consumer interactions: the time-consuming process of contacting local businesses, government offices, or corporate customer support. Users frequently face tedious automated phone trees, prolonged hold times, and unhelpful departmental transfers when trying to schedule appointments, request service changes, or ask routine questions. Muse functions as an automated proxy equipped with natural speech synthesis and conversational reasoning, enabling it to navigate interactive voice response (IVR) systems, state user intents, and complete administrative transactions without requiring real-time human intervention.

Architecturally, the calling mechanism pairs speech-to-text and text-to-speech pipelines with LLM-driven agentic workflows. When a user requests a call, Muse formulates a plan, dials the target telephone number over traditional telecommunications networks, and interprets incoming audio streams dynamically. It maintains context throughout long conversations, providing appropriate answers based on user-provided profile parameters or explicit instructions.

However, the technology faces operational hurdles during real-world execution. Many automated call screening systems and human receptionists routinely disconnect inbound calls upon recognizing synthetic voice patterns or AI disclaimers. To overcome these reception barriers, conversational models are increasingly fine-tuned to mirror human cadence, pauses, and conversational nuances, making voice detection more difficult for recipients. Moving forward, Meta aims to expand Muse’s autonomous agent ecosystem by introducing dedicated email addresses for users and integrating deeper protocol support across personal productivity tasks.

Who's involved: Meta, Alexandr Wang (Chief AI Officer), Muse, and competing platforms like Instinct and Google Gemini.
